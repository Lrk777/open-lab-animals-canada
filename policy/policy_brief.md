# Canada's animal research data gap

*Open Lab Animals Canada, policy brief, 2026-11-10*

## Summary

Canada reports less about animal-based science than the UK, the EU or the US, and what it reports is hard to
use. Primate use is rising. Most of it is regulatory testing, not university neuroscience. Fixing
transparency is cheap, has precedent abroad, and is something the research sector itself has called for.

## Key findings

- **Primate use is rising.** 8,889 nonhuman primates were reported in 2025, +103% since 2012,
  with a peak of 12,138 in 2023. Cynomolgus macaques (6,552) dominate; rhesus macaques, the species in
  the York University investigation, numbered 190.
- **Most primate use is regulatory testing.** 51% of primate uses in 2025 were for regulatory
  testing and 19% for fundamental research.
- **Per person, Canada uses primates at US levels and several times the European rate.** About
  214 per million people in 2025 (177 counting only laboratory species in higher
  invasiveness categories), against 179 in the US, 27 in Great Britain and 10 in the
  EU (2023). Counting rules differ, but the gap with Europe survives the conservative comparison.
- **Canada ranks last on transparency.** On a 12-dimension index Canada scores 41/100, against
  87 (GB), 88 (EU) and 53 (US). Canada ranks last in 74% of 10,000 random
  re-weightings and never ranks in the top two.
- **The official data have labelling problems.** An audit found 167 labelling and structure
  issues (species renamed between years, primates recorded without a species, a mislabelled 2025 file).
- **Published papers omit key welfare details.** In 326 open-access Canadian primate research papers (2010-2026, mostly neuroscience), housing conditions were reported in 17%, whether fluid was controlled or unrestricted in 15%, fluid amounts or monitoring in 10%, and the number of surgeries per animal in 2%. Reporting did not change detectably after ARRIVE 2.0 (2020). (Automated baseline; human validation in progress.)
- **Funding.** CIHR and NSERC awards that name primates as study animals averaged about $8M a year in 2020-2024, against about $27M for projects using organoids, organs-on-chips, stem-cell and other non-animal methods (keyword classification; see methods).

## Recommendations

1. **Publish institution-level statistics.** The US already publishes per-facility reports. The CCAC
   could publish counts by certified institution, starting with primates, dogs and cats.
2. **Report actual severity, not only prospective categories**, as in the UK and the EU.
3. **Report primate origin**: supplier country, generation, and whether animals are reused.
4. **Create a Canadian Openness Agreement**, as the CCAC itself proposes, modelled on the UK Concordat.
5. **Make ARRIVE 2.0 reporting a condition of CIHR/NSERC-funded animal research**, including housing and
   fluid-control details for primates.
6. **Track replacement funding**: report annually how much CIHR/NSERC money goes to non-animal methods.
